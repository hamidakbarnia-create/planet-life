## 1. Purpose

### 1.1 Role of Legal & Compliance Policies

This document defines the permanent governance framework for METIORO's legal and compliance obligations. It establishes policy-level commitments that all METIORO capabilities, communications, and operations must uphold.

This document governs *policy intent* — not legal implementation. Specific privacy notices, terms of service, cookie policies, and jurisdiction-specific compliance measures belong in dedicated legal documents and operational systems.

### 1.2 Relationship to Higher Authority

Legal & Compliance Policies are subordinate to the METIORO Constitution and Level 1 documents. Where legal obligation and constitutional principle align, both apply. Where conflict arises between operational convenience and compliance, compliance prevails unless resolved through formal governance review.

### 1.3 Scope

This document covers governance-level policy commitments only. It does not contain legal advice, country-specific regulations, GDPR implementation details, or operational runbooks.

## 2. Privacy

### 2.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO commits to handling personal and sensitive user data with respect for confidentiality, purpose limitation, and user rights. Privacy is a trust requirement (Trust Architecture §2) and a constitutional obligation.

### 2.2 Governance Requirements

1. Data collection must be limited to what is necessary for declared purposes.
2. Users must be informed of what data is collected and why.
3. Data retention must be governed, not indefinite by default.
4. User rights regarding their data must be respected and addressable.
5. Third-party data sharing must be governed, documented, and constrained.

Implementation of privacy controls belongs in Engineering Standards, operational procedures, and dedicated legal documents — not in this policy framework.

---

## 3. User Consent

### 3.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO commits to obtaining appropriate user consent where required by applicable law and governance policy. Consent must be informed, specific, and revocable.

### 3.2 Governance Requirements

1. Consent requests must clearly state what the user is consenting to.
2. Consent must not be bundled with unrelated obligations.
3. Withdrawal of consent must be as accessible as granting it.
4. Capabilities requiring consent must not operate without valid consent where required.
5. Consent records must be maintainable for accountability purposes.

Specific consent mechanisms, UI patterns, and storage methods are implementation details outside this document.

---

## 4. Transparency

### 4.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO commits to transparency about its operations, data practices, analytical methods, and limitations — consistent with the Transparency trust principle and Brand Identity Standard.

### 4.2 Governance Requirements

1. Users must be able to understand what METIORO does with their data.
2. Analytical methods must not be deliberately concealed from users.
3. Material changes to data practices or service terms must be communicable to affected users.
4. Limitations of the platform must be honestly represented in legal and product communications.

Transparency obligations support but do not replace explainability requirements for individual recommendations.

---

## 5. Explainability

### 5.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO commits to explainability as both a trust requirement and a compliance-oriented obligation. Users must be able to understand the basis of guidance that materially affects their decisions.

### 5.2 Governance Requirements

1. Important recommendations must be capable of explanation as defined in Trust Architecture §4.
2. Legal and product communications must not contradict explainability commitments.
3. Opaque or oracular presentation of guidance is prohibited at the policy level.
4. Explainability failures that affect user rights or safety must be addressable through governance review.

Detailed explainability standards may be defined in a future Level 2 or Level 3 document.

---

## 6. Responsible AI

### 6.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO commits to responsible use of intelligent and automated components. AI-assisted capabilities must meet the same trust, explainability, evidence, and human authority standards as deterministic capabilities.

### 6.2 Governance Requirements

1. Automated outputs must not override human decision authority.
2. AI-assisted reasoning must not be presented as infallible or supernatural.
3. Limitations of automated components must be acknowledged.
4. AI capabilities must be reviewable and accountable under governance processes.
5. AI trust failures must be treated with the same severity as product or scientific trust failures.

Specific AI evaluation frameworks and quality metrics may be defined in future extensions referenced by Trust Architecture §9.

---

## 7. Jurisdiction

### 7.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO acknowledges that it may operate across multiple jurisdictions and that legal obligations may vary by user location, data residency, and service delivery context.

### 7.2 Governance Requirements

1. Jurisdiction-specific obligations must be identified and registered as Regulatory requirements in the Requirement Registry.
2. This governance document does not prescribe jurisdiction-specific rules.
3. Where jurisdictions conflict, resolution requires qualified legal review and governance approval.
4. Service availability and data handling in specific regions must conform to registered regulatory requirements.

Country-specific implementation documents are outside the scope of this policy framework.

---

## 8. Regulatory Evolution

### 8.1 Policy Commitment

METIORO commits to monitoring and adapting to evolving regulatory landscapes affecting personal data, automated decision support, and digital services — without compromising constitutional trust principles.

### 8.2 Governance Requirements

1. Regulatory changes that affect METIORO must be assessed for impact on this policy framework.
2. New regulatory requirements must be registered in the Requirement Registry under the Regulatory category.
3. Policy updates must be versioned and traceable.
4. Regulatory compliance must not be used to justify erosion of user agency, explainability, or transparency.

Operational response to regulatory change belongs in Level 5 Operational Procedures.
