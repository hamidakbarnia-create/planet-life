## Preamble

METIORO exists so that important personal decisions can be made with clearer structure, better timing awareness, honest uncertainty, and preserved human authority.

The world already has entertainment astrology, chatbots, coaching slogans, and opaque advice. METIORO exists because those forms fail the person who must live with the consequences of a real decision.

This Product Constitution is the DNA of the product. It does not describe screens. It does not name technologies. It does not promise a roadmap. It defines what METIORO is, what it may never become, and how every future judgment about the product must be made.

If a builder memorizes only this document, they should still build METIORO correctly.

**Relationship to higher authority.** The METIORO Constitution remains supreme. This Product Constitution elaborates product identity and product law. In conflict, the METIORO Constitution prevails. **Object model, evaluation pathway, Evaluation Contract, state machine, repository, and evidence eligibility** are consolidated in [ACR-0001](../architecture/ARCHITECTURE_CONSOLIDATION_RESOLUTION.md); this Constitution states product laws, ACR makes them implementation-unambiguous. After ratification, amendment of this document requires the same gravity as constitutional change: written proposal, recorded decision, explicit owner approval, and version increment.

## Article I — Existence

### I.1 Why METIORO Exists

METIORO exists to improve the quality of personal decisions under uncertainty.

People face irreversible or costly choices — commitment, exit, investment, timing, relocation, conflict, care — with incomplete information, competing pressures, and unreliable advice. METIORO exists to give those people a disciplined instrument for evaluation, not a substitute for judgment.

### I.2 The Permanent Problem

The permanent problem is **decision quality under uncertainty**.

Not loneliness. Not entertainment. Not conversation. Not prediction of fate. Not productivity theater.

Forever, METIORO exists to help a person answer, with structure and honesty:

> Given who I am, what I know, what I do not know, and when I might act — what is a better move, why, with what trade-offs, and with what confidence?

### I.3 Category

METIORO is **Personal Decision Intelligence**.

It is not an astrology app.
It is not a chatbot.
It is not a general AI assistant.
It is not a life coach.
It is not a horoscope generator.

Any feature, message, or architecture that would make METIORO primarily one of the above is a constitutional violation.

### I.4 The Primary Object

The primary object of METIORO is the **Decision Case**.

A Decision Case is a durable unit of personal decision work: created, enriched, evaluated, compared, planned, executed, reflected upon, archived, and reused.

Conversations, calendars, profiles, people, files, and notifications exist only insofar as they serve Decision Cases.

## Article II — Permanent Identity

### II.1 What Must Never Change

These commitments are permanent:

1. The user owns every decision.
2. The system is advisory, never authoritative over the user’s life.
3. Recommendations require explanation.
4. Uncertainty must be exposed, not hidden.
5. Evidence precedes opinion.
6. Structure precedes language.
7. Timing is part of decision intelligence, not decoration.
8. Trust precedes capability expansion.
9. Category fidelity to Personal Decision Intelligence.
10. Rejection of mystical, prophetic, and entertainment-horoscope identity.

### II.2 What Must Never Be Added

METIORO must never add, as core product identity:

1. Fate, prophecy, or guaranteed outcomes.
2. Chat as the primary product metaphor.
3. Unstructured general assistance disconnected from Decision Cases.
4. Opaque scoring presented as truth.
5. Features whose sole purpose is engagement without decision value.
6. Module-local recommenders that bypass the single decision pathway.
7. Coercive language that implies the user must comply.
8. Mystical or occult framing.
9. Capabilities that cannot meet explainability and uncertainty standards.
10. Anything that fails Rule Zero (Article III).

### II.3 What Should Always Remain Deterministic

The following must remain governed by deterministic product logic — not by free generative improvisation:

1. Decision Case identity and lifecycle state.
2. Mapping of a decision to its reasoning family.
3. Required and missing information.
4. Evidence eligibility and rejection.
5. Timing evaluation structure (relative favorability, ranking, windowing).
6. Constraint and dependency application.
7. Decision driver dimensions and scorecard structure.
8. Stance formation rules feeding recommendations.
9. Confidence level calculation inputs and penalties.
10. Output Contract completeness checks.
11. Safety and overclaim bans.
12. Fail-closed rejection of invalid recommendation packages.

Language may be assisted. **Authority over decision structure may not be improvisational.**

### II.4 Where Intelligence Assistance May Be Trusted

Assisted intelligence may be trusted to:

1. Help classify intent into a Decision Case type.
2. Help gather and organize context.
3. Synthesize structured evaluation artifacts into clear language.
4. Explain drivers, trade-offs, and uncertainty already computed.
5. Suggest missing information that would improve precision.
6. Help the user draft plans the user still controls.

Assistance is subordinate to structure, evidence, and Output Contract.

### II.5 Where Intelligence Assistance Must Never Decide

Assisted intelligence must never:

1. Own the user’s decision.
2. Invent a recommendation stance without structured evaluation.
3. Suppress uncertainty to sound confident.
4. Fabricate evidence.
5. Bypass deterministic gates.
6. Claim certainty about life outcomes.
7. Quietly alter Decision Case history.
8. Act as an autonomous life agent without explicit user authority and bounds.

**The engine evaluates. The user decides. Language explains. Language does not rule.**

## Article III — Rule Zero

> **Every capability must improve the lifecycle of a Decision Case.**
> If it cannot, it must not exist.

Rule Zero is the product’s deletion principle. Growth that violates Rule Zero is decay.

## Article IV — Foundational Principles

### Principle 1 — Human Authority
The user owns every decision. The system never owns decisions.

### Principle 2 — Evaluation Without Certainty
The system evaluates options. It never claims certainty about life outcomes.

### Principle 3 — Mandatory Explainability
Every recommendation must be explainable. An opaque recommendation is not a recommendation; it is a defect.

### Principle 4 — Mandatory Uncertainty
Every recommendation must expose uncertainty. Hidden doubt is a trust violation.

### Principle 5 — Quality Over Volume
Decision quality matters more than response length, novelty, or conversational fluency.

### Principle 6 — Timing Completeness
A recommendation about when to act, without timing assessment, is incomplete when timing is material to the decision.

### Principle 7 — Trade-off Completeness
A recommendation without trade-offs is incomplete. Gain without cost is marketing, not intelligence.

### Principle 8 — Structured Authority
Generative language never decides. Structured evaluation decides what may be recommended; language may only express it.

### Principle 9 — Decision Case Centrality
Everything revolves around Decision Cases. Surfaces that do not serve Cases are peripheral or invalid.

### Principle 10 — Lifecycle Necessity
Features that do not improve Decision Cases must not exist.

### Principle 11 — Evidence Primacy
Claims that affect decisions require evidence. Opinion must never masquerade as analysis.

### Principle 12 — Progressive Precision
Confidence rises only with relevant evidence. Missing information must reduce confidence, not be papered over with style.

### Principle 13 — Relative Timing Honesty
Timing intelligence ranks and compares favorability. It does not decree destiny.

### Principle 14 — One Pathway
There is one decision pathway for decision-bearing guidance. Parallel shadow recommenders are forbidden.

### Principle 15 — Trust Before Expansion
No new intelligence is worth eroding trust. If trust requirements cannot be met, the capability waits or dies.

### Principle 16 — Agency Preservation
The system may advise, warn, and structure. It may not coerce, shame, or imply moral failure for disagreement.

### Principle 17 — Consistency of Commitment
The same constitutional commitments apply across languages, surfaces, and time. Local exceptions are violations.

### Principle 18 — Learning Without Prophecy
The system may learn from outcomes to calibrate. It may never rewrite the past as if it had predicted it.

### Principle 19 — Premium Means Evaluable
Premium is not ornament. Premium is clarity, structure, comparability, and respect for the user’s time and stakes.

### Principle 20 — Decade Stability
Principles outlive tools. No short-term tactic may redefine identity.

## Article V — Definitions That Bind

These definitions are normative. Product quality is judged against them.

### V.1 Product Quality
Product quality is the degree to which METIORO improves decision quality while preserving agency, trust, and honesty about limits.

Vanity metrics that do not track decision quality are not product quality.

### V.2 Premium Experience
A premium experience is one in which the user can:

- open a real Decision Case quickly,
- see what is known and unknown,
- receive structured evaluation,
- compare options or times,
- understand why and why not,
- see confidence and how to improve it,
- leave with an actionable plan they still control.

Premium is density of useful structure, not abundance of words, animation, or mystique.

### V.3 Trust
Trust is the user’s justified belief that METIORO:

- will not overclaim,
- will show its reasoning,
- will respect agency,
- will treat sensitive context with care,
- will remain consistent with its identity,
- will fail closed rather than invent authority.

Trust is earned by restraint as much as by capability.

### V.4 Explainability
Explainability means a decision-bearing output can answer, from system artifacts:

1. Why this recommendation?
2. Why not the alternative(s)?
3. What reduced confidence?
4. How could accuracy improve?
5. What assumptions were used?

If these cannot be answered, the output must not ship as a recommendation.

### V.5 Decision Quality
Decision quality is the improvement in the user’s ability to choose among options with clearer criteria, visible trade-offs, appropriate timing awareness, and honest confidence — relative to deciding without the system.

METIORO does not define decision quality as “the user obeyed the recommendation.”

### V.6 Timing Quality
Timing quality is the clarity, comparative honesty, and constraint-awareness with which the system evaluates when to act.

High timing quality means:

- relative ranking is intelligible,
- windows are distinguishable from noise,
- constraints are respected,
- uncertainty is stated,
- no claim of guaranteed life outcome is made.

### V.7 Evidence
Evidence is documented, eligible, reviewable support for analytical claims, with declared limits.

Not evidence:

- rhetorical confidence,
- unverified anecdote presented as fact,
- mystical assertion,
- generative invention.

### V.8 Confidence
Confidence is a disclosed estimate of how strongly the current evidence and completeness support the recommendation package.

Confidence is not charisma.
Confidence is not certainty.
Confidence must fall when material information is missing or conflicting.

### V.9 Failure
Failure includes any of the following:

1. A recommendation without explanation.
2. A recommendation that conceals uncertainty.
3. A claim of outcome certainty.
4. A bypass of the single decision pathway.
5. A feature that does not serve Decision Cases.
6. Mystical or prophetic framing.
7. Coercion of user agency.
8. Fabricated or ineligible evidence used for decision-bearing claims.
9. Generative language inventing stance.
10. Inconsistency that silently changes constitutional commitments.

Delight that produces these failures is still failure.

## Article VI — Non-Negotiable Rule Domains

### VI.1 Engineering Rules

1. Decision-bearing outputs must pass structured validation before presentation.
2. Invalid recommendation packages fail closed.
3. Provenance from inputs to recommendation must be preservable.
4. Deterministic product logic must not be replaced by unconstrained generation.
5. Sensitive personal context must be purpose-limited.
6. Historical Decision Case evaluations are not silently rewritten.
7. Consistency of constitutional behavior matters more than local cleverness.

### VI.2 Product Rules

1. The Decision Case is the system of record.
2. Rule Zero governs all feature admission.
3. Category fidelity is mandatory.
4. Engagement features that do not improve decisions are rejected.
5. Depth beats breadth when they conflict.
6. Incomplete high-stakes guidance must prefer honesty over completeness theater.
7. Product copy must remain calm, scientific, explainable, never mystical.

### VI.3 AI Rules

1. Assistance may classify, synthesize, and explain under constraint.
2. Assistance may not own decisions.
3. Assistance may not invent evidence.
4. Assistance may not suppress uncertainty.
5. Assistance may not bypass structured evaluation.
6. Autonomous action on a user’s life requires explicit bounded authority from the user and remains advisory unless the user executes.
7. Model or vendor change must not change constitutional behavior.

### VI.4 UX Rules

1. The product must feel like a decision instrument, not a chat toy.
2. Structure must be visible before prose.
3. Uncertainty and confidence must be easy to find.
4. Trade-offs must be visible when a recommendation is shown.
5. Improving accuracy must be offered when confidence is limited.
6. The user must be able to reject or revise without friction or shame.
7. Ornament must never substitute for evaluability.

### VI.5 Architecture Rules

1. One decision pathway for decision-bearing guidance.
2. Reasoning families scale decision types; one-off shadow engines do not.
3. Timing, personal context, constraints, and drivers are first-class evaluation concerns.
4. Output completeness is contractual, not stylistic.
5. Surfaces are clients of Decision Cases, not independent intelligence products.
6. Future channels (mobile, voice, agents, collaboration) reuse the same object model and pathway.
7. Architecture may evolve; constitutional obligations may not silently evaporate.

### VI.6 Governance Rules

1. Locked constitutional decisions remain binding until formally amended.
2. Lower documents may elaborate; they may not contradict.
3. Trust requirements veto capability ambition.
4. Regulated or high-harm domains require heightened restraint and clearer limits.
5. Identity claims in public communication must match product reality.
6. Amendment requires explicit approval, record, and versioning.
7. Convenience is not grounds for constitutional exception.

### VI.7 Decision Intelligence Rules

1. Evaluation precedes recommendation language.
2. Drivers and trade-offs are mandatory for complete recommendations.
3. Missing information is a first-class state.
4. Comparison and alternative stance are part of intelligence, not extras.
5. Decision quality is the objective, not obedience.
6. Related decisions and dependencies matter when they materially affect choice.
7. Reflection and learning may follow action; they must not falsify prior honesty.

### VI.8 Timing Intelligence Rules

1. Timing speaks in relative favorability, not destiny.
2. Compare and search modes must explain ranking.
3. Constraints can eliminate dates; they must say so.
4. A forced early action under urgency must still expose cost.
5. Timing without personal or situational context has lower confidence and must say so.
6. Beautiful calendars that do not serve Decision Cases fail Rule Zero.
7. Timing quality is measured by clarity and honesty, not by dramatic certainty.

### VI.9 Future Compatibility Rules

1. New surfaces must obey the same Product Laws.
2. New intelligence methods must submit to explainability and evidence standards before affecting recommendations.
3. Collaboration and multi-person contexts must preserve individual agency and clear authority.
4. Marketplace or extensibility mechanisms must be governed; they may not import mystical or opaque decision packs as first-class truth.
5. Agents may watch, remind, and prepare; they may not seize ownership of decisions.
6. Decade validity: avoid baking transient tools into identity.
7. If a future capability cannot satisfy this Constitution, METIORO forgoes it.

## Article VII — The Twenty Product Laws

These laws are timeless. They avoid tools, vendors, models, and implementation. They bind the next decade.

### Law 1 — Ownership
The human owns the decision. The product never does.

### Law 2 — Advice, Not Authority
METIORO advises. It does not command, shame, or replace judgment.

### Law 3 — Case First
If it does not serve a Decision Case, it does not belong.

### Law 4 — Structure Before Words
No recommendation language may outrun structured evaluation.

### Law 5 — Explain or Withhold
What cannot be explained cannot be recommended.

### Law 6 — Uncertainty Is Product
Concealed uncertainty is a defect; disclosed uncertainty is integrity.

### Law 7 — Evidence Gates Claims
Decision-bearing claims require eligible evidence and declared limits.

### Law 8 — Confidence Tracks Truthfulness
Confidence follows completeness and agreement of evidence, never tone.

### Law 9 — Trade-offs Are Mandatory
A recommendation that hides what is lost is incomplete.

### Law 10 — Timing Is Material
When time affects the decision, timing assessment is part of the work.

### Law 11 — Relative, Never Prophetic
Timing and scoring compare favorability. They do not prophesy outcomes.

### Law 12 — One Pathway
All decision-bearing guidance shares one governed pathway.

### Law 13 — Agency Is Sacred
The user may accept, modify, defer, reject, or ignore. The product remains respectful.

### Law 14 — Trust Outranks Novelty
A clever capability that damages trust is forbidden.

### Law 15 — Precision Is Earned
More intimate or consequential claims require more complete context.

### Law 16 — Consistency Is Identity
The same commitments hold across surfaces, languages, and time.

### Law 17 — Learning Without Myth
Outcomes may calibrate the system. They must never be rewritten into prophecy.

### Law 18 — Premium Is Clarity
The premium standard is evaluable clarity under stakes — not spectacle.

### Law 19 — Fail Closed
When integrity cannot be met, the system withholds or degrades honestly rather than inventing authority.

### Law 20 — Permanence of Purpose
METIORO exists to improve personal decisions under uncertainty. That purpose does not expire with fashion.

## Article VIII — Tests of Compliance

Before any feature, architecture change, intelligence capability, or roadmap item is accepted, it must pass:

1. **Category Test** — Does this keep METIORO as Personal Decision Intelligence?
2. **Case Test** — How does this improve a Decision Case lifecycle?
3. **Agency Test** — Does the user still own the decision?
4. **Explainability Test** — Can why / why not / uncertainty / improvement be answered?
5. **Evidence Test** — What eligible evidence supports decision-bearing claims?
6. **Certainty Test** — Does any language overclaim outcomes?
7. **Pathway Test** — Does this bypass the single decision pathway?
8. **Trust Test** — Would shipping this make a careful user trust METIORO less?
9. **Premium Test** — Does this increase evaluable clarity, or only noise?
10. **Decade Test** — Would this law still make sense in ten years without renaming tools?

Failure of any test is grounds for rejection.

## Article IX — Ratification and Amendment

### IX.1 Ratification
Upon owner acceptance, this Product Constitution becomes binding product law for METIORO builders, partners, and governing documents beneath it.

### IX.2 Amendment
Amendment requires:

1. Written proposal stating the article affected, the change, and the rationale.
2. Record in the Decision Log.
3. Explicit project owner approval.
4. Version increment.
5. Publication of the amended text.

Silent drift is prohibited. Convenience is not an amendment process.

### IX.3 Interpretation
When interpretation is disputed, choose the reading that best preserves:

human authority,
explainability,
uncertainty honesty,
Decision Case centrality,
and category fidelity.
